- **Web: reject software/fallback adapters under `CpuFirst::Auto`, recover from transient WebGPU device loss, and expose `App::on_gpu_fallback` / `runegpufallback`.**
  - Under `CpuFirst::Auto` on full-window web pages, software/fallback WebGPU and WebGL2 adapters (`DeviceType::Cpu` / `GPUAdapterInfo.isFallbackAdapter` and SwiftShader/`llvmpipe`/Lavapipe/WARP metadata) are rejected before creating a device or locking the canvas, keeping the page on `CpuCanvas` (while allowing `--features webgl` builds to try a hardware WebGL2 adapter if WebGPU is software). `CpuFirst::Off` (`RUNE_CPU_FIRST=0`), `GpuLossPolicy::Terminal`, `App::web_document`, and inline `App::canvas_id` pages continue accepting software adapters.
  - Under `GpuLossPolicy::Recover`, WebGPU `device.lost` now performs bounded adapter/device re-acquisition (3 losses per 5-minute forgiveness window with backoff, matching Chromium and native `SurfaceRetries`): full-window `CpuFirst` pages draw on `CpuCanvas` while recovering and hand back over when restored, while `GpuFirst` / inline / document pages pause in place (`ContextLost`) until restored or exhausted. `GpuLossPolicy::Terminal` remains latched without recovery or CPU fallback.
  - Added `App::on_gpu_fallback`, `GpuFallback`, and `GpuFallbackStage` (`Init`, `Shader`, `HandoverTimeout`, `DeviceLost`), paired with a bubbling `runegpufallback` DOM `CustomEvent` (`detail: { stage, reason }`) on `canvas[data-rune-canvas]`, so split-bundle WebGPU/WebGL2 web hosts can unmount and swap to WebGL2 on a fresh canvas.
