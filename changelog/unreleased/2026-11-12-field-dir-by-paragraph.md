- **A multi-line text field with `El::dir_by_paragraph` lays each line out in its own direction, and typing in a long draft reshapes only the line it changes.** As a `contenteditable` with `dir="auto"` and `unicode-bidi: plaintext` does, an Arabic or Hebrew line is right-aligned and runs right to left beside an English one; an empty line, or one of only digits and punctuation, runs left to right; a mention pill's label is isolated, so `@src/app.ts` stays left to right on an Arabic line and the line's direction skips it; and the field's own `El::dir(Direction::Auto)` comes from its text outside its pills, left to right while it's empty, its placeholder laid out in the placeholder's own direction with the field's padding mirrored to it. One field, one caret and one selection cross the lines: the arrows move across each line as it's drawn and leave it the way its paragraph runs, the caret keeps its side where two runs meet, Up and Down keep their column through shorter lines and lines of the other direction, Home and End go to the line's ends, a selection's line break shows at the end its line runs to, and hit testing, the IME's caret, spelling marks and accessibility text follow each line. Each line is its own Parley layout (`TextLayout::Paragraphs`), kept across edits: an edit shapes the lines it changed and keeps the rest from the field's last layout, so a keystroke in a 2,000-line mixed draft takes 10,212 to 12,184 µs of the UI thread for the whole frame (it took 313,739 to 342,917 µs), 158 to 245 µs of it shaping the one line (211,195 to 237,531 µs). Off macOS a word forward (Ctrl+Right) lands at the next word's start, past the spaces, as Chromium's does. On Windows spell checking runs on a thread of its own: `ISpellChecker` answers from another process, a call per misspelled word, which held the frame 40 to 1,600 ms a keystroke; a word's marks show once it's back, and a line whose words are all checked keeps its marks, so a key in a long draft looks up only the line it changed.
