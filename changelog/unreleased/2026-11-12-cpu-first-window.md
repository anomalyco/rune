- **Windows: a window's first frame is drawn on the CPU and on screen before its GPU exists, live from that frame; the GPU takes over when it's ready, with no visible change.** An opaque window opens with no GPU: the CPU renderer paints frame 1 into a DIB section the size of its display (one row length, so a resize repaints only what moved; the first frame's rows are touched on a worker while it's laid out), the window shows cloaked, takes the frame in one `BitBlt` per changed rect, uncloaks and then activates. Input, hover, scrolling and motion run through the runtime as on the GPU, and each later frame copies its changed rects' bounding box in one `BitBlt` (one copy per rect tore 1-15 frames in 600 on the visible desktop; one per frame, none). After frame 1 a below-normal thread makes the GPU and its swap chain on a DirectComposition visual nobody shows yet; both renderers draw the same frames until a GPU frame has finished, then the visual is committed and the CPU buffer is freed after counting how many of its pixels the GPU's frame drew otherwise (2 of 576,000, by at most 1/255). The UI Automation adapter is made after frame 1 unless a screen reader runs. On the visible desktop (a 982 x 656 window, Iris Xe, 150%): on screen 75-103 ms after launch against 187-251 ms waiting for the GPU; no composed frame changed at the hand-over in 24 of 24 launches; a resize step while the CPU draws blocks the caller 3,900 µs (p50) against 51,500 µs on the swap chain, with no unfilled frame. `RUNE_CPU_FIRST=0` turns it off; a window opens on the GPU as before when it's transparent or another window already has a GPU.
