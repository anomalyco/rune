- **Windows: a notched mouse wheel glides as Chromium's does, and a wheel at an inner viewport's end moves the one around it.** Whole notches (`WheelEvent::notch`, set for whole-line deltas on Windows) that no `on_wheel` handler takes now ease to where they send a viewport on Chromium's mouse-wheel curve (`cc/animation/scroll_offset_animation_curve.cc`): `cubic-bezier(0.42, 0, 0.58, 1)` over 200 ms for up to 120 device pixels, down to 100 ms at 480, sampled at every frame step. A notch that comes while it moves sends it on from where it is, keeping its speed, so stacked notches run as one motion and a notch back turns it round; a hand, a key, a fling or `Tx::scroll_to` take over at once. Vertical and horizontal viewports, the horizontal axis's motion now one `XMotion` (rest, momentum, glide, wheel) rather than a velocity and an optional glide. Reduced motion jumps, as does an app that calls `Runtime::set_wheel_glide(false)`; macOS, Linux, precise trackpads and free-spinning wheels move 1:1 as before. A wheel's delta now starts on the innermost viewport under the pointer that still has room in its direction, so a short region inside a page no longer traps the wheel at its end.
