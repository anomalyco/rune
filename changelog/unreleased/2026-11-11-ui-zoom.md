- **`Cx::zoom` zooms the view as a browser's page zoom does: the window's scale times the zoom.** The view lays out in the window's points over the zoom at the window's scale times it, as Chromium folds a page zoom into the device scale, so borders, icons, glyphs and pixel snapping land on the device grid at every zoom (a 1 pt border at 125% on a 1.5x display is one device pixel, as Chromium draws it), and every flight, morph, presence and spring moves in the one space the layout is in. An app that zoomed with `El::scale` on its root saw what moves draw at the unzoomed size and snap to the zoomed one when it landed. A new zoom lays the view out again in the commit that sets it. The host still speaks window points: `Runtime::set_viewport` takes the window's size and scale, pointer, touch and wheel input arrive in the view's points (the wheel turns as far on screen, a pinch is the same gesture), and what goes back to the window (the IME area, pop-up and window menus, drag images, `on_screen_layout` rects, a fitted height, the Windows caption buttons, which keep the window's size) is in window points; `Cx::chrome_insets` and `Cx::titlebar_inset` report the view's. rune-kit's `TabStrip::zoom` and `WindowTitlebar::from_insets`'s zoom go, as a zoomed view's points need no conversion.
