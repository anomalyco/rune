- **Windows: a playing clip draws no UI frame per picture.** While a clip plays and its place on screen holds still, its decoder thread scales each picture into the clip's own NV12 swap chain (made as Chromium makes its video swap chains, on a DirectComposition surface handle) and presents it at the picture's time, on a visual under the window's UI; the UI punches the clip's place out of its pixels, so corners, clips, opacity and anything drawn over the clip stay the UI's own. While the place moves (scrolling, a resize, a fade) the UI draws the pictures, as before: a swap chain's present and a DirectComposition commit land on different compositions, and moving the layer frame by frame showed seams in 85% of composed frames. The UI's swap chain composites premultiplied only while a layer shows (DWM blends what the UI presents then). What's New over a busy home screen: 84 → 30 frames a second, the rest the screen's own spinners. `RUNE_VIDEO_LAYER=0` keeps every clip in the UI.
- **Windows video waits for Rune's GPU.** A window drawn on the CPU first makes its GPU after its first frame; a clip that opened before then decoded on the CPU for good. It now waits for the GPU (up to 3 s, while its source resolves), and one that still started on the CPU opens again on the GPU, at its picture, once there is one.
- **Layers: a clip of whole pixels with square corners is a scissor.** Such a group draws straight into its parent instead of a target of its own, and an inline clip inside another takes both.
