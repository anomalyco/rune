- **Linux: `App::titlebar` is the window's only bar on GNOME.** A Wayland compositor without `xdg-decoration` (GNOME's) draws no window frame, so winit drew its own grey bar above the app's titlebar. A window with `App::titlebar` now opens without it there: Rune draws the caption buttons the desktop's `button-layout` names (`org.gnome.desktop.wm.preferences`, through the settings portal, followed while the app runs: GNOME's default `appmenu:close` is close alone, a layout on the left puts them at the left, and all three sit at the right where no portal says) as round buttons over the band, reports their room and the band's height in `Cx::chrome_insets`, and resizes the window from a 5 point band inside its edges (16 points along each side of a corner for the diagonals), with the resize cursors. `.window_drag()` regions move the window, maximize or restore it on a double click and open the compositor's window menu on a right click; a maximized or full-screen window has no resize band, a side the compositor tiled has none either (`Cx::tiled_edges`), full screen hides the buttons, and a button the window can't act on (`Cx::window_actions`: maximize on a fixed-size window, minimize where the compositor has none) isn't drawn, as GTK's window controls leave it out. The buttons' symbols take `Cx::caption_color`, else the system's light or dark setting, as on Windows. X11 and compositors that draw the frame themselves (KDE, Sway, Hyprland) are unchanged, as are macOS and Windows. `RUNE_LINUX_FRAME=0` keeps winit's frame, and `RUNE_LINUX_FRAME=1` draws Rune's under any Wayland compositor.
