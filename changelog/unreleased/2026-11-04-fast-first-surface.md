- **A first surface on a new GPU device shows much sooner on Windows, and an app can make the device ahead of a show.**
  - **Precompiled uber shader:** on DX12 the uber pipeline loads FXC bytecode Rune ships (`uber.vs.dxbc`, `uber.fs.dxbc`) instead of compiling the WGSL through FXC on every new device: about 250 ms less per device (Renderer::new on an RTX 5070 Ti: 2 ms instead of 250 to 330 ms). The bytecode is used only while the WGSL and layout hash as when it was made, and a pipeline the driver refuses compiles the WGSL. `uber_dxbc_is_fresh` (Windows) checks it against the HLSL wgpu generates; `RUNE_REGENERATE_DXBC=1` rewrites it. `RUNE_DISABLE_DXBC=1` compiles the WGSL as before. Devices on Windows ask for `PASSTHROUGH_SHADERS` when the adapter has it.
  - **The DX12 check happens on the first GPU use:** whether a DX12 adapter exists is asked then rather than as the event loop starts, on `warm_gpu`'s thread when the app warms ahead of a show. A cold first show without `warm_gpu` enumerates the DX12 adapters twice (the check, then `gpu/pick.rs`'s selection), each making a device on every adapter, and pays roughly 150 ms more. A windowless app touches no GPU until it draws.
  - **`rune::warm_gpu(features)` and `warm_gpu_with(features, prepare)`:** start making the device and renderer the next window or overlay surface would make, on a thread, on a hint that one will show (a shortcut's first key); the surface takes them, waiting for the thread if needed, and unclaimed they go half a minute after the last call. `prepare` runs on that thread with the device, for an app's own pipelines. They do nothing while a surface's device lives, and nothing on macOS (`prewarm_gpu`).
  - **`ConfiguredOverlay::release_gpu_after(duration)`:** `release_gpu_when_hidden` once the overlay stayed hidden that long, so a burst of shows keeps the surface and an app at rest gives the device back.
  - **`GpuEvent::Drew`** also follows `warm_gpu`'s offscreen pass on the device it makes.
