- **Wayland: a decorated window's content rounds its bottom corners under the client-side frame.** The adwaita frame's headerbar rounds the top two and its shadow has always cut a 10-point rounded window, but the content's surface was square, so the bottom two corners showed past the frame's one-point border, and the border's straight bands crossed the shadow's rounded cut-out. The frame's border now follows the same arcs, its shadow reaches over the window's foot so the crescents the arcs cut out of it are drawn, and the shadow below the window is the same pixel for pixel as a square frame's (`vendor/sctk-adwaita`, a new `[patch.crates-io]` entry, square while maximized, tiled or fullscreen like the headerbar); `WindowExtWayland::content_corner_radius` and `has_client_side_frame` say what radius the content leaves it and whether its decorations are a client-side frame's at all — true in fullscreen too, where the frame draws square corners (`vendor/winit`, its own patch entry too). A window whose decorations the frame draws keeps its surface's alpha — premultiplied, or square corners — and fades the pixels outside those two arcs by their coverage (`window::corners`, one draw of two corner squares a frame); a window under a compositor that draws the decorations itself (KDE, sway, Hyprland) keeps exactly the surface and opaque region it had, since the frame is never in use. Rune's own window stays opaque throughout: same clear colour, same device, and winit keeps the window an opaque region except the two corner squares a frame rounds. Windows, macOS, borderless windows, X11 and a surface with no alpha mode paint exactly what they did.
