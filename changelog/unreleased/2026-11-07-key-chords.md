- **Key chords as data: `Chord` and `Keybind`.** `Chord::parse("mod+shift+t")` matches a `KeyEvent` by the keys held on the platform, with `mod` the primary modifier (⌘ on macOS, Ctrl elsewhere) and `ctrl`, `alt`, `shift` and `super` literal keys, so "ctrl+tab" is ⌃⇥ on a Mac and Ctrl+Tab elsewhere. A test such as `cmd && !ctrl` never passes on Windows and Linux, where Ctrl sets both; a chord does. `Keybind::new("mod+b").on(KeyPlatform::Windows, "alt+home")` binds per platform, and `label()` reads "⇧⌘T" on macOS and "Ctrl+Shift+T" elsewhere. `Shortcut` and script chords take `mod` too. On Windows, Ctrl+←/→ and Ctrl+Backspace in a text field move and delete by word instead of to the line's edge, and Ctrl+Alt+letter reaches `on_key` instead of arriving with no key.
